# Attendance Regulations

These regulations apply to every student enrolled in an undergraduate
programme of the College. They are issued by the Office of the Dean
(Academics) and supersede all earlier attendance circulars.

## Purpose

Regular attendance is a condition of eligibility for the end-semester
examinations. The College records attendance so that a student who is
falling behind can be warned early, while there is still time to recover.

## Minimum Attendance Requirement

A student must attend **at least 75% of the periods held** in a semester to
be eligible to write the end-semester examinations.

Attendance is computed as the number of periods attended divided by the
number of periods held, taken across all courses registered for in that
semester. It is not rounded: 74.9% is below the requirement.

## How Attendance Is Counted

The College timetable has six teaching periods in a working day. A day of
absence therefore counts as six periods missed.

A student who arrives more than ten minutes after the start of a period is
marked absent for that period. Attendance is recorded by the course teacher
and published to students every week. A student who believes a period has
been recorded wrongly must raise it with the course teacher within five
working days of publication; after that the record stands.

Only working days count. Weekends and the holidays declared in the College
calendar are not periods held, so they neither add to nor reduce a
student's percentage.

## On-Duty (OD) and Attendance

On-Duty leave is granted for activities undertaken on behalf of the
College — inter-college technical and cultural events, sports
representation, NSS and NCC camps, and placement drives arranged by the
Training and Placement Cell.

**Periods covered by approved OD are counted as attended.** OD therefore
does not reduce a student's attendance percentage. Because the periods are
added to both the periods held and the periods attended, OD will usually
raise the percentage of a student who is below 100%.

OD counts as attendance only once it has been approved by the faculty
advisor. A request that is submitted but not yet approved, or that is
rejected, does not count.

## Medical and Family-Emergency Leave

Medical leave and family-emergency leave are **not** counted as attendance. The
periods missed are added to the periods held and not to the periods
attended, so the percentage goes down.

Leave is still worth applying for: approved leave is recorded as authorised
absence, which the College takes into account when considering condonation
(see Shortage and Condonation). Unapplied absence is not.

## Shortage and Condonation

A student whose attendance is below 75% is said to have a **shortage of
attendance**.

### Condonation band (65% to below 75%)

A student whose attendance is at least 65% but below 75% may apply for
condonation of the shortage. Condonation is considered only where the
shortfall is substantially due to approved medical leave supported by a
certificate from a registered medical practitioner. It is granted by the
Head of the Department on the recommendation of the faculty advisor, and a
condonation fee is payable.

Condonation may be granted **no more than twice** during the whole
programme.

### Below 65%

A student whose attendance is below 65% is not eligible for condonation.
They may not write the end-semester examinations for that semester and must
re-register for the courses concerned when they are next offered.

## Leave Requests That Would Cause a Shortage

When a student applies for leave, the College's systems show the effect the
leave would have on their attendance. A request that would take a student
below 75% is **not refused automatically**. It is flagged for the faculty
advisor, who decides whether to approve it as an exception. An exception
granted for a leave request does not by itself condone a shortage at the end
of the semester; condonation is a separate decision, made as above.

## Warnings

Students whose attendance falls below 80% at any point in the semester
receive a written warning, copied to their parent or guardian. A second
warning is issued below 75%.
